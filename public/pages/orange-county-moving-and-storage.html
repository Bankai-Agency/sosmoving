Moving and Storage in Orange County, One Crew From Pickup to Return

SOS Moving runs moving and storage in Orange County as a single job: the crew that loads your home also drives the load to our climate-controlled facility and, once the next place is ready, brings every piece back and sets it in the rooms you name. The way the service works in general is laid out on our storage services page, and a move with no storage stop is covered on our Orange County movers page.

Combining the two cuts down on handling. A rented unit means you load a truck, unload it into the unit, load it again weeks later and unload once more at the new address. With us the furniture is wrapped once, listed once and carried by people who saw how it was packed.

Nothing waits at your curb or on your driveway between the two moving days, which matters in a county where some cities put a clock on containers parked in the street. Storage can be booked on its own, for a garage clear-out or a remodel, or as one leg of a full relocation.

Short-Term, Long-Term or In-Transit: Matching Storage to Your Dates

Your calendar picks the storage type; the crew and the facility stay the same.

Short-term storage

Anywhere from a few days up to several weeks. It bridges a lease that ends before the next one begins, the weeks a house is staged for buyers, or a short remodel. We collect on your last day at the old address and deliver as soon as you hold the new keys.

Long-term storage

A month or longer: a remodel that is still waiting on approvals, a year spent working abroad, furniture that will not fit a smaller home, business files or spare inventory. The term can be extended at any point without a penalty, so an optimistic finish date never turns into a trap.

In-transit storage

Built for households leaving Orange County for another state whose new home is not ready on arrival day. The load travels from your driveway to our facility, waits there and then continues to the destination, so there is no second storage company to hire at the far end. The interstate leg is priced as a flat rate, as described on the long-distance moving page.

Orange County Storage Situations We Plan Around

Parking & permits: a pod on a Huntington Beach street runs on a clock

Huntington Beach lets a portable storage pod stand on a private driveway with no permit, as long as it stays off the sidewalk and the street. Move it to the curb and the rules tighten: a city permit, a width of eight feet or less, a limit of seven days, and no spot in front of a driveway, bus stop, accessible curb ramp, fire hydrant, colored curb or crosswalk.

That suits a weekend project, not a remodel. With our service nothing is left outside after pickup day, because the truck leaves loaded and the goods go into the facility. What we ask from you is a clear driveway or legal curb space close to the door for the loading hours; the wrapping, the carrying and the inventory are on us.

HOA, condo & building rules: overnight passes in Laguna Woods Village

Laguna Woods Village requires an overnight parking pass for any non-resident vehicle that stays inside the community overnight. Residents can arrange a pass for a visiting vehicle for up to two weeks, and for up to 60 days a year through Resident Services, so the rule is workable, but it has to be set up ahead. Our own practice is simpler: a loaded SOS truck does not spend the night inside a gated community.

If a move-in slips, the load comes to our facility for the night at the $200 overnight storage rate, or into short-term storage if the delay grows. Before pickup, ask the association how a moving crew is admitted at the gate and which hours it allows for moves, and let us know if a certificate of insurance is on its list, since that document costs $50 from us.

Area specifics: Old Towne Orange remodels and a county of renters

In the Old Towne historic district of the City of Orange, most exterior changes to a property are reviewed against the Historic Preservation Design Standards for Old Towne. Many projects are cleared by city staff through Minor Design Review, while substantial ones go to the Design Review Committee. If your remodel needs that step, put the furniture into long-term storage instead of betting on a return date, and extend the term without a penalty if the work runs on.

In older houses we pad door frames and cover floors before the first piece is carried. Countywide, DataUSA puts owner-occupied homes at 56.4% of occupied housing in 2024, which leaves roughly four households in ten renting; for them the usual trigger is a lease that ends before the next one starts.

Logistics & timing: fitting both legs into 8AM – 6PM

Every crew drives down from our Los Angeles base at 5530 Jillson Street, so travel is part of the bill: road time beyond the first 10 miles counts twice, and once the address is 30 or more miles out, the minimum rises to 4 hours. Pickups and returns are booked inside our Monday – Sunday, 8AM – 6PM day, and a morning start leaves room for loading plus the drive to the facility before the day closes.

The return is a separate trip under the same rules, so plan it for a day when the new home is fully open to us. A same-day pickup is possible when a crew is free, and the delivery date can be set by phone once your keys or approvals are in hand.

When Orange County Households Need Storage Between Moves

Storage is rarely the goal of a move; it fills a gap in the calendar. The gap may be a few days between two leases, a few months while a kitchen is rebuilt, or the stretch between selling one house and closing on the next. Naming the gap you actually have is the first step toward booking the right service.

Renting is a big part of the picture here. With roughly two households in five renting, many storage calls start with a lease: the old one ends on the 31st, the new one begins on the 5th, and those five days need a home for a sofa, a bed and forty boxes.

Owners have other triggers. A remodel that needs rooms empty, a house staged for sale with half its furniture pulled out, a downsizing move into a smaller place, or a job transfer out of state with nothing lined up yet at the destination.

Each case comes down to two questions: how long, and how firm is the end date? The answers set the storage type, and the type decides how the pickup and the return are planned.

Short-Term, Long-Term or In-Transit: Choosing the Right Term

Short-term storage is measured in days or weeks, not months. Choose it when the end date is fixed and close: keys to an apartment on a known day, a floor refinish with a contractor's finish date, or a buyer's walkthrough after which the staging pieces return.

Long-term storage starts at a month and has no set ceiling. It suits projects whose finish depends on someone else, such as a city review, a contractor's crew or a sale that has not closed. Since the term can be extended without penalties, an honest estimate is safer than a hopeful one.

In-transit storage belongs to interstate relocations. Your load leaves the house, waits at our facility and continues to the new state when the home there is ready, so you avoid hiring a second storage company at the destination. The route and the flat-rate price for that leg are worked out together with our planning for long-distance moves from Orange County.

Whatever the type, the rate card, the minimums and the 8AM – 6PM working day apply to both trips; time spent in storage changes none of them.

If you are torn between two types, describe the uncertainty on the first call. The estimate plans for the likely date, and stretching past it later takes nothing more than a phone call.

What Belongs in Climate-Controlled Storage

The facility keeps temperature and humidity steady year-round. That protects the belongings most likely to suffer in a hot garage or a damp shed: solid-wood and veneer furniture, leather, mattresses, electronics, paper files, photographs and framed art.

Things we accept:

  • household furniture, bed frames and mattresses;
  • appliances, cleaned and fully dry;
  • televisions, computers and other electronics;
  • boxed belongings, books and documents;
  • artwork and seasonal decorations;
  • business inventory and office equipment.

What stays behind, for safety and legal reasons: fuel, paint thinner and other flammables, corrosives, explosives, propane tanks, batteries, food that spoils, live plants, and things that invite pests or mold. When you are unsure about an item, raise it during the estimate rather than on pickup day.

A little preparation pays off later. Defrost and dry a refrigerator at least a day before the crew comes, drain a washer, and put the documents you might need soon into one clearly marked carton, so a partial delivery can find them without unpacking half a unit.

Storage for a Remodel, a Sale, a Smaller Home or a Business

A remodel is the most common reason owners store for longer than a few weeks. Empty rooms let contractors work faster, keep plaster dust off upholstery and put the dining table out of reach of ladders and saws. Book the pickup a day or two before demolition starts rather than on the morning the contractor arrives.

Selling a house works differently. Some sellers take out part of the furniture so the rooms photograph larger and feel emptier on showings. In that case we collect only the pieces you mark, leave the staged rooms as they are, and later deliver everything to the next address in a single trip.

Downsizing sits somewhere between the two. When a four-bedroom household moves into two bedrooms, deciding what to keep can take longer than the move itself. Storage buys time to sort without paying for the old home any longer than needed, and the term stretches without a penalty until the sorting is finished.

Business files, stock and office equipment

Storage is not only for households. Small companies use it for archived records, seasonal stock and furniture from an office that has shrunk or sits between two leases. The list of accepted items is the same, and so is the written inventory.

Label every carton on two sides with a short description and a number that matches your own spreadsheet. When someone later asks for last year's tax boxes, the numbers point straight to them, and a partial delivery takes minutes instead of an afternoon of opening cartons.

During a full office relocation, storage can hold whatever the new space cannot take yet while everything else moves in. Our office moving team for Orange County plans both parts together, inside the same 8AM – 6PM working day.

Pickup, Inventory and Return: The Crew's Side of the Job

On pickup day the crew starts with protection, not lifting. Furniture goes into moving blankets, glass and lacquered surfaces get padding under stretch wrap, and hanging clothes transfer straight into wardrobe boxes. Every piece is written on an inventory before it goes onto the truck.

From there the truck heads directly to the facility. Items are placed so they cannot shift or lean on one another during the term, which matters far more over three months than over three days.

If you would rather not pack the small things yourself, add packing help in Orange County to the same booking. Cartons in small, medium and large sizes cost from $4 each, you pay only for the ones used, and tape and stretch wrap come free in every estimate.

The return is an ordinary moving day run in reverse. You call with a date, the crew takes your items out of storage, drives them to the new address and puts each piece in the room you choose. Need something back early? A single carton or one piece of furniture can come out ahead of the rest; we arrange access to the facility or a partial delivery.

Comparing Moving and Storage Companies in Orange County

Search results mix three different businesses: storage lots where you rent a unit and haul everything yourself, container firms that drop a box at your address, and full-service movers that store for you. Compare them on one yardstick: who loads, who drives, and how many times your furniture changes hands.

  • Is the space climate-controlled, and does that cover humidity as well as heat?
  • Who wraps and lists the items, your crew or me?
  • If my end date moves later, is there a charge for extending?
  • How is drive time billed for an address this far from your base?
  • Where will a container or truck stand, and for how long, under my city's rules?
  • Which licenses cover the job, inside California and across state lines?

Planning the Calendar for Moving and Storage in Orange County, CA

Work backward from the day you need everything back. If the next home sits under an HOA or a building manager, ask early about permitted moving hours, elevator or loading-area reservations and a certificate of insurance. The COI costs $50, and we send it to the manager once you give us the details.

Crews work Monday – Sunday from 8AM to 6PM. A morning pickup fits the whole first leg, loading plus the drive to the facility, inside that window, while a call late on a busy afternoon may push loading to the next morning. Same-day pickup happens whenever a crew is free.

A simple countdown keeps the dates honest:

  • two weeks ahead: the estimate call, the inventory, the storage type and both dates;
  • one week ahead: HOA or building paperwork, the COI request, gate access and a spot for the truck;
  • two or three days ahead: packing finished, the refrigerator defrosted, and an essentials bag set aside that travels with you, not with us;
  • pickup day: a walk-through with the crew lead and a check of the inventory before the doors close;
  • return: a call with the delivery date once the new home is ready for furniture.

Gated communities need a little extra lead time. Arrange entry for the crew in advance and check whether the community has its own overnight vehicle rules; Laguna Woods Village, for one, requires a pass for a non-resident vehicle parked there overnight. We sidestep the question by never leaving a loaded truck inside the gates overnight.

Moving within the county and skipping storage altogether? Then you need only local moving across Orange County, booked by the hour on the same rate card.

What Shapes the Final Bill

A storage job has two moving legs and one storage term. Each leg is billed by the hour, so the usual factors apply: how much you own, how far the truck parks from the door, stairs and elevators, and how much is packed before the crew walks in.

Distance adds a layer of its own. Past the first 10 miles, every minute of driving from our base is billed twice, and an address at least 30 miles out raises the floor of the booking to 4 hours instead of 3. The estimate confirms which rule fits your address, so no one has to guess from a web page.

The storage term is priced by unit size and availability. A tighter inventory means a smaller unit, so the cheapest hour you can spend is the one before pickup: sell, donate or discard whatever will not follow you into the next home.

Two habits shorten both legs. Gather everything that goes into storage in one or two rooms before the crew arrives, and keep whatever stays behind clearly apart, with a note on the door or a strip of tape. The crew then carries without stopping to ask, and the clock runs on lifting rather than on questions.

Fixed extras come straight from the rate sheet: gas $30, the $50 deposit that counts toward your total, a COI at $50, an extra truck at $200 and overnight storage at $200. Overtime begins after 8 hours on a single job.
