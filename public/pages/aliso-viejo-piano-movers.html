Parking & Permits: What Counts as an Oversized Truck

The city code treats a vehicle longer than 24 feet, or heavier than 10,000 pounds, as oversized, so a 26-foot box truck counts as one. Such a vehicle may not stand on a city street between 2:00 and 6:00 a.m., or for more than 12 hours in a row during the day, on two consecutive days.

The exception is what matters on move day: the rule does not apply while the truck is actively loading or unloading, or while it is parked to serve a job on a property in the same block. The city describes no temporary permit for this case, so our truck stays worked rather than left standing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the piano billed apart from the rest of my Aliso Viejo move?

No. It is one hourly job with four movers and a truck, plus a flat $200 for the instrument. The three-hour minimum covers the whole booking, and an address 30 or more miles from our Los Angeles base lifts that minimum to four hours.

Why does the crew always arrive as four?

The $200 instrument charge is only written onto a four-person booking. Two movers take the low end of the board, one steadies the high end and one goes ahead clearing the route, which is what keeps a board level on an entry that steps down.

Where will your truck stand on my street?

Street type decides that. Local streets allow curbside parking; on the busy four-lane roads such as Glenwood Drive the city warns that curbside parking may not be suitable, especially where a bike lane shares the road, so we load from a garage or a side street and allow more carry time.

My neighborhood is gated with private streets — what do you need?

The gate procedure, the name of the sub-association and any certificate of insurance requirement. On a private street the association's rules govern rather than the city parking code, while enforcement on public streets runs through the Orange County Sheriff's Department, which the city contracts for police services.

Can the piano move on the day I call?

Often, yes, because same-day work happens whenever a four-person crew has a gap. Our hours run Monday to Sunday, 8AM to 6PM, so the instrument, the rest of the load and the drive all have to fit inside that window.

Will you tune or store the instrument afterwards?

No. SOS carries pianos; tuning, valuation, disposal and instrument storage sit outside the service. Book a technician separately for a date after the move, once the piano is standing where it belongs.

Piano Movers in Aliso Viejo, CA Work on an Hourly Clock

An instrument is not sold here as a product with its own price list. It is a line inside an hourly household move: four movers, one truck, a flat $200 for the piano and a three-hour minimum that belongs to the booking as a whole. That framing decides almost everything else about the day.

Rates follow the day of the week and the way you pay. Monday to Thursday, a four-person crew with a truck runs at $199 an hour in cash or $209 by card; Friday to Sunday the same crew is $215 and $225. A $30 gas line and a $50 deposit, credited back against the bill, complete the fixed part.

Distance carries rules of its own. Thirty or more miles between our Los Angeles base and your address lifts the minimum from three hours to four, driving beyond ten miles is billed at double time, and the hours after the eighth count as overtime.

None of that is specific to pianos, which is exactly the point: the instrument sits inside the same clock as the sofa. Where a building asks for a certificate of insurance, that is $50 and is arranged before the crew rolls.

People sometimes expect a piano-only call to be cheaper than a household day, and on paper the difference is small. The same four movers, the same truck and the same minimum apply either way, which is why an instrument travelling alongside beds and boxes uses time that was already booked.

A deposit of $50 holds the slot and comes straight off the closing figure. Everything else is read off the clock: arrival, walk-through, protection, the carry, the drive and the set-down at the far end.

Four Movers, and Why the Number Is Fixed

A crew of four is not a preference, it is the condition on which the $200 charge is written. Four people mean a cabinet can be lifted, held and steered at the same moment instead of being walked and rested, and one of the four is deliberately not carrying.

This city gives that fourth pair of hands plenty to do. The General Plan describes hillsides, ridgelines, canyons and view corridors, with ground rising from about 120 feet above sea level in the south to more than 890 feet at Moulton Peak in the north.

Across terrain shaped like that, the low end of the board is often a step or two below the high end for the whole carry. Two movers take the low end, one steadies the top, and the fourth goes ahead opening doors, shifting planters and calling the turns.

The same arithmetic applies in a lift lobby. A board cannot be tipped inside a car with a low ceiling, so somebody has to hold the angle while the doors are blocked and the instrument is walked in.

Gates change the arithmetic again. Where a private street or a guard controls the entrance, one mover deals with access and the spotting while the other three stay with the cabinet, and the crew does not shrink to cover the errand.

What the $200 Instrument Charge Actually Buys

It is not a handling fee in the abstract. The money pays for the specialist part of the job: the padded piano board, the belts that hold the cabinet to it, the rub rails and ratchet straps that give the piano its own anchor in the truck, and the extra person on the crew.

Before any of that touches the instrument, the case is closed and padded. Lid and fallboard go down, blankets cover the cabinet, and shrink holds the blankets where they belong so nothing slides during a turn or a set-down.

Floor protection follows, and it is not an afterthought. Runners go over tile, hardwood and any shared corridor, because a loaded board and four pairs of work boots do more to a floor than the piano ever does to itself.

A grand is partly dismantled for the trip. Lid, lyre and legs come off, the body travels on its straight side, and the parts go back on in the room you have chosen rather than wherever the truck was unloaded.

What the charge does not buy is worth stating. Tuning, valuation, disposal and instrument storage are not services we sell, and we do not take pianos on long-haul runs; the $200 buys the board, the hardware and the fourth mover, nothing more and nothing less.

Getting the House Ready Before the Truck Arrives

Preparation is mostly measurement and photographs. A side-on shot of the instrument, one of the doorway it must leave through, one of every flight of steps and one taken from the street towards the entrance tell a crew leader more than a paragraph of description.

Clear the route the day before if you can. Rugs rolled, planters off the path, the spot where the piano will stand left empty: a cabinet set down twice takes twice as long, and the clock does not pause while furniture is shuffled.

Tell us about the gate before the day, not at the gate. Where a sub-association controls entry the crew and truck need to be expected, and where the building wants a certificate of insurance that $50 document has to exist in advance.

Last, decide about the rest of the load. If the household is moving too, the same four movers handle both, and the piano takes its place in the loading order instead of requiring a second visit.

Small things save real minutes on the invoice. Pets behind a closed door, children out of the carry path, a space kept free at the curb where the truck can stop, and somebody at the destination who can say where the instrument finally stands.

Steps, Slopes and Corridors: the Local Version

Street type quietly sets the difficulty of a carry. Local streets here are quiet two-lane roads with curbside parking allowed.

That is the easy case, where the truck stands close and the board runs straight in. Collector streets are also two lanes, but parking is restricted near intersections that have a separate right-turn lane.

Addresses on the main four-lane roads are different again. Aliso Creek Road between SR-73 and Glenwood Drive, Glenwood Drive itself and Pacific Park Drive north of Wood Canyon Drive are divided roads, and the city warns that curbside parking may not be suitable on the busier segments, especially where a bike lane shares the road.

Secondary roadways do provide curbside parking, except near intersections with left-turn striping: Aliso Creek Road north of Glenwood Drive, Aliso Viejo Parkway south of Pacific Park Drive, and Wood Canyon Drive from Pacific Park Drive to Aliso Creek Road.

One stretch is the exception the plan names itself. Wood Canyon Drive south of Aliso Creek Road is still striped as a two-lane undivided road, to be brought up to the County four-lane secondary classification when needed, so a crew working an address off it puts a spotter behind the tailgate.

There is a parking rule behind all of this. A truck longer than 24 feet or heavier than 10,000 pounds counts as oversized under the city code, and a 26-foot box truck is over that length, so where it stands and for how long is not a matter of taste.

The limits are plain: no oversized vehicle left on a city street between 2:00 and 6:00 a.m., and no more than 12 hours in a row during the day, on two consecutive days. A vehicle that is actively loading or unloading is exempt.

The toll corridor is a choice rather than a rule. SR-73 roughly bisects the city from northwest to southeast, cash collection ended on 14 May 2014, and a driver without an account pays online against the plate number within five days either side of the trip.

Professional piano movers in Aliso Viejo do not guess any of this on the morning itself. Answer the walk-through questions honestly when you book, and the estimate stops being a guess.
