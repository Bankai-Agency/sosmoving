How a Glendale Office Relocation Runs, Stage by Stage

What Glendale Streets and Buildings Expect From an Office Move

Addresses a mile apart behave nothing alike here. Four things get checked before a date is confirmed: where the truck stands, what the building demands on paper, what the surrounding district does all day, and how the hours fall.

Parking & permits: the truck at the door, the crew in a garage

Downtown has city garages the crew can use. Marketplace, Exchange and Orange Street give 90 minutes free to every visitor, then charge $3.00 per hour with a daily maximum of $15.00, so personal cars go there for the shift while the truck keeps the loading zone or the dock.

On the street, and in some off-street lots, payment runs through multi-space meters that cover 8–10 spaces each and are tied to the space number rather than the vehicle. Our crew notes that number on arrival and keeps the receipt, because a van arriving later pays for its own space.

Businesses in Montrose carry an extra layer: the city runs a Montrose/Sparr Heights employee parking permit program with its own application and map. We ask which permits your staff hold before settling on where the truck stands.

Building rules: COI, freight elevator, access window

Downtown high-rises hold corporate headquarters including Age of Learning, ServiceTitan, Whole Foods, Cisco and Avery Dennison, and management offices in that class of building treat paperwork as a condition of entry. Converted storefronts and ground-floor suites bring the opposite problem, because there is no dock at all and the whole job crosses a public sidewalk. The route is planned, frames and floors are covered, and the walkway stays passable while the crew works.

Planning an Office Move in Glendale Before the Truck Is Booked

The first document is a list, not a quote. Walk both floors with a notebook and count desks, chairs, cabinets, screens, server racks, storage rooms and whatever lives in the corridor, because that total decides crew size, truck count and the hours you end up paying for.

Numbering comes straight after the list. Each desk gets a number matching a position on the new floor plan, every carton and cable bag carries the same number, and the crew places furniture by that number instead of asking where things go.

A purge before move day is the cheapest part of the project. Archives that only need to exist rather than be reachable can be boxed and stored, and broken chairs or dead monitors are better disposed of than carried, since everything in the truck is billed by the time it takes to handle.

Equipment deserves its own pass. Photograph the back of each machine, label the ports, note which screens belong to which seat, and agree with your own engineers what they will shut down and what our crew will carry.

Lead time closes the planning stage. A small suite can often move the same day a crew is free, while a full floor with a booked elevator and a certificate of insurance wants its date set well ahead.

Notices belong on the same checklist as cartons. Carriers, utilities, the alarm company, the cleaning contractor, signage and anyone who delivers to you all need the new address and the switchover date, and that list is far easier to write two weeks out than on the last afternoon.

Paperwork opens the door. Most managed buildings want a certificate of insurance naming the landlord and the management company before they release a freight elevator, and ours costs $50 and follows the wording your property manager supplies.

Expect a named access window, a booked elevator, protection for lobbies and corridors, and a dock slot measured in hours rather than days.

Shopping-center tenants work to a different timetable. Smaller buildings ask for less, though never for nothing. A converted storefront may have no dock and no service lift, and then the real constraints are the sidewalk, the front door and how long a truck may hold the curb.

Keeping the Business Open While the Office Moves

Downtime is the true cost of a commercial move, and it never shows up on the mover invoice. A staged plan keeps most of your people working while the building empties around them.

Stages are ordered by how little the contents are needed day to day: storage and archives first, then departments that can work from laptops for a shift, then the desks that must be live the next morning.

Weekend stages take the parts that cannot move while staff are seated. Crews work Saturday and Sunday inside the same hours as any other day, and the higher Friday-to-Sunday rate is worth spending only on the stage that genuinely needs it.

Phones, printers and meeting rooms travel last. A floor without a working conference room loses less than a floor without desks, so the schedule protects the seats first and the extras afterwards.

Someone should own the plan on your side as well as ours. When the building manager, the IT lead and the crew all deal with the same two people, a stage that slips by an hour becomes a phone call instead of a lost day.

Two shorter dates often beat one long one. Splitting a floor across a weekday and a weekend keeps the hourly rate down on the bulk of the load, leaves a buffer if a freight elevator is released late, and gives your team a working office on both sides of the gap.

Furniture, Files and Equipment on Moving Day

Office furniture is built to come apart, and it survives the trip better that way. Benching frames, sit-stand desks, panel systems and conference tables are stripped, wrapped, carried flat and rebuilt at the new address by the crew that dismantled them.

Filing is decided by the building rather than by preference. Lateral cabinets can be rolled loaded where a dock and a lift allow it, and get emptied into numbered cartons where the route runs through a lobby and out a front door.

Electronics travel packed, not stacked. Monitors go in cartons, cables into labeled bags, rack hardware upright, and disconnecting and reconnecting computers joins the estimate when your coordinator adds the service.

A few items change the crew instead of the packing. A safe, a plotter or any single piece over 300 lbs is quoted at $150 and handled by three movers, a second truck is $200, and goods that must stay loaded overnight are $200.

Protection is the quiet part of the day. Masonite on lobby floors, pads on door frames and guards on corridor corners are what the building remembers long after the furniture has gone.

A short list of items never goes on the truck. Solvents, aerosols, fire extinguishers, gas cylinders and similar hazardous goods stay with you or go to a specialist, and we hand over the full list during planning so nothing is discovered at the loading dock.

The answers change street by street, which is why the walkthrough happens before the number.

Driving between them is a plan of its own. Signal timing has been synchronized for up to 50 intersections along Central Avenue, Brand Boulevard, Glendale Avenue, Broadway and Colorado Street, and those corridors carry a loaded truck far better than the residential grid on either side.
