How Much Do Movers Cost in Los Angeles?

How Much Do Movers Cost in Los Angeles?

Last Updated: 
Thursday, October 29, 2026

Table of Contents

    The moving cost in Los Angeles for a local move comes down to three numbers: how many movers you book, how many hours they work, and which day of the week it is. Everything else on the bill is small and fixed. So if you want a quick answer to "how much do movers cost in LA," here it is: a studio with two movers and a truck typically lands at $387–$465, and a three-bedroom house with four movers at $1,224–$1,605, depending on the day, how you pay and how long the job runs. Below is where those numbers come from, what pushes them up, and what you can actually control.

    What Los Angeles movers charge per hour

    Local movers in LA bill by the hour, and the hourly rate depends on crew size. Here is the rate card we use at SOS Moving, because a real rate card is more useful than a national average:

    Crew (with truck)Monday–Thursday, cash / cardFriday–Sunday, cash / card

    Two things jump out. Friday is already priced as a weekend day. And paying by card adds $10 an hour. Neither sounds like much, but over a six-hour job with three movers, Saturday-by-card instead of Tuesday-in-cash is $156 more for the exact same work. The full rate card, with every fee, is on our local moving page.

    How that compares with what others report

    Published figures for the cost of movers in Los Angeles are all over the place, and part of the reason is that they don't measure the same thing. Some quote a rate per mover, others per crew:

    • MoveAdvisor (March 2026): $90–$150 per hour per mover.
    • Let's Get Moving (May 2025): $110–$140 per hour on average.
    • Move & Care (2026): $100–$170 per hour for a two-person crew with a truck.

    Read the unit before you compare. "$90 per mover" for a crew of two is $180 an hour, which is a very different number from "$90 an hour." It's an easy way for two quotes that look close to end up hundreds of dollars apart.

If your home is big enough to push past a full day, a bigger crew can be the cheaper option: a fifth mover adds $40 an hour, and that can cost less than the overtime it prevents.

    For context, here is what other sources say LA moves cost overall. Piece of Cake Moving puts the average local LA move at about $1,050 (studio around $550, 1+ bedroom $900, 2+ bedrooms $1,200, a large home $2,000). iMoving reports an average local move of $1,250 across its LA bookings for the last 90 days. Those are market averages from other companies, not SOS prices, but they're a useful sanity check: if a quote for a one-bedroom is far above $1,000, ask what's in it.

    What else ends up on the bill

    Hourly labor is most of the cost. The rest is a short list, and it's worth knowing before moving day rather than reading it on the invoice:

    • Minimum time: 3 hours, or 4 hours if your move is 30+ miles from our base. A small move that takes 90 minutes is still billed as 3 hours.
    • Gas: a flat $30.
    • Double drive time: when the pickup and drop-off addresses are more than 10 miles apart, drive time counts twice.
    • Overtime: after 8 hours, at 1.5 times the hourly rate.
    • Stairs, long carries, multiple stops: no separate charge. They're included in the hourly rate.
    • Deposit: $50 to hold the date, and it counts toward the bill, so it isn't an extra cost.
    • Extra truck: $200, for loads that don't fit in one.
    • Overnight storage: $200, if move-out and move-in aren't on the same day.
    • Certificate of insurance (COI): $50, if your building asks for one.
    • Piano: $200, and it needs a crew of 4.
    • Any item over 300 lbs (a safe, a solid-wood armoire): $150, with a crew of 3.

    Notice the last two. The heavy item sets the crew size, and the crew size sets the hourly rate. A piano doesn't just add $200; it means you're paying the four-mover rate for the whole job.

    Why LA moves take longer than they look

    On paper, a two-bedroom across town is a few hours of carrying. In Los Angeles, the clock runs for reasons that have nothing to do with your furniture.

    Traffic and distance

    The 405 at 4 p.m. doesn't care that you're paying by the hour. A move from the Westside to the Valley can spend more time on the freeway than on loading, and when the two addresses are more than 10 miles apart, that drive time is billed double. Early starts help more than most people expect.

    Parking and long carries

    In Hollywood, Koreatown, Downtown and much of West LA, the truck often can't stop at the door. We don't charge a separate long-carry fee, but every extra minute walking a dresser down the block is still billed time. If your street needs a temporary parking permit, getting it sorted early is cheaper than paying a crew to circle.

    Buildings with rules

    Many LA apartment buildings require an elevator reservation, a set move-in window, or a certificate of insurance from the mover. None of this is hard, but a crew waiting 40 minutes for a freight elevator is 40 minutes on your bill. Call both buildings a week ahead and ask what they need.

    Stairs and hillside homes

    Third-floor walk-ups in Silver Lake, canyon houses with a long driveway: the same couch takes longer. With us there's no stair fee on top; the extra time is simply part of the hourly bill. Some movers do charge per flight: Move & Care, citing HireAHelper data, puts flat stair fees at $50–$70 per flight beyond the first. Either way, it's why two moves of the "same size" can differ by an hour or two.

    Season and day

    MoveAdvisor and NerdWallet both point to late spring through summer as the busiest moving season, with October to April quieter. In LA, end-of-month is busy year-round because that's when leases turn over. Our own rates are higher Friday through Sunday and may vary at the beginning and end of the month, so the lowest rate on our card is Monday–Thursday in cash, and if your dates are flexible, it's worth asking about the middle of the month.

    Long-distance moves are priced differently

    Moving out of LA rather than across it? The hourly math above doesn't apply. We treat any move over 150 miles as long distance, and those are quoted as a flat rate after an estimate: one price for a dedicated 26-ft truck, with fuel, mileage and taxes included. The price depends on how much you're moving, how far, and any special services. Details are on our long-distance movers page.

    As market context only: MoveAdvisor puts long-distance moves from LA at roughly $3,000 to well over $10,000, depending on distance and home size, and iMoving reports a range of $1,400 to $11,200. That spread is exactly why a long-distance number needs your inventory, not a guess. For one of the most common routes, see our breakdown of what a move to San Francisco will cost.

    How to pay less for an LA move

    Most savings come from cutting hours, not from haggling over the rate. A few things that genuinely move the number:

    1. Pick Monday–Thursday, mid-month if you can. With any crew size, Monday–Thursday is $16 an hour less than Friday–Sunday. Rates may also vary at the beginning and end of the month, so ask about the dates in between.
    2. Pay cash. $10 an hour less than card, every hour.
    3. Be fully packed before the crew arrives. Half-packed kitchens are the classic way a four-hour move becomes six.
    4. Book the right crew. Too small a crew on a big home stretches the day toward overtime. Too big a crew on a studio wastes money. If you're unsure, describe the home on the phone and ask.
    5. Group small jobs into one trip. Each booking has its own 3-hour minimum, so three separate furniture runs cost far more than one.
    6. Sort parking and elevators ahead. Waiting time is billed time.
    7. Get rid of things first. Less stuff, fewer hours. Simple, and it works.

    If money is the main constraint, our guide to moving to LA on a budget has more ideas, and if you're still deciding on neighborhoods, start with what to know before moving to Los Angeles.

    Quick answers

    How much does it cost to hire movers in LA for a small move?

    With two movers on a Monday–Thursday, the 3-hour minimum plus gas comes to $387 in cash or $417 by card. That's the floor for a local booking with us within 30 miles of our base, even if the job takes less time.

    Is it cheaper to move on a weekday in Los Angeles?

    Monday through Thursday, yes. Friday is priced like the weekend, so "weekday" isn't quite the right rule. Think Monday–Thursday.

    Does the $50 deposit add to the cost?

    No. It holds your date and is applied toward the final bill.

    How do I get an exact price?

    For a local move, the final price is the hours the crew actually works at the rate for your day, plus the fixed fees above. The more precisely you describe your home, access and big items when you call, the closer the estimate will be.
