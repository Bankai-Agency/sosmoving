What Santa Ana Streets and Buildings Expect From an Office Move

Public parking around the Orange County Civic Center sits in numbered lots. P1 at 1020 West Civic Center Drive costs $0.50 per 15 minutes to a $15 daily maximum, while P2, P3, P4, P6, P7 and P8 charge $1 per 15 minutes to a $20 maximum. Three of them close on weekends and major holidays: P3 at 690 West Civic Center Drive, P4 at 630 North Broadway Street and P7 at 1002 West Santa Ana Boulevard.

P5 at 211 West Santa Ana Boulevard is a meter with a two-hour limit per stay. City garages hold longer: Garage B at 300 East 5th Street, Garage C at 310 North Birch Street and Garage D at 420 North Main Street run automated round the clock, free for 60 minutes and then $1.75 an hour to a $15 cap.

Which of them is usable is checked by the foreman before the truck is positioned.

Building rules: the COI, the freight lift and the dock slot

Management offices ask for paperwork before they ask for a date. The certificate of insurance a management office asks for costs $50 from us and names whichever entities they list, and the freight lift and dock window are booked in the same exchange rather than left to move day. Where a suite has no dock, the crew works from the nearest legal stopping point and carries.

Street work is a different animal. Encroachment permits for work in the street or public right of way come from the Public Works Agency Development Engineering office, and the permitted list covers lane or sidewalk closures supporting street, utility or maintenance work; an applicant shows a City of Santa Ana business license and a certificate of insurance, plus a contractor’s license and approved plans. That is construction paperwork, and we say so when a manager raises it.

Area specifics: the Civic Center block and the Hutton Centre towers

Questions About Moving an Office in Santa Ana

How much does an office move in Santa Ana cost?

Business moves are billed by the hour and the crew size sets the rate: two movers with a truck are $119 cash or $129 card Monday to Thursday, $135 or $145 Friday to Sunday. Three movers cost $159 or $169 and $175 or $185; four movers $199 or $209 and $215 or $225.

Three hours is the standard minimum, and four hours applies once the address sits 30 miles or more from base. Beyond 10 miles the drive time counts double, and the $50 booking deposit is credited against the invoice.

Our building management wants a certificate of insurance — do you provide one?

Yes. Ours costs $50 and reaches the management office ahead of move day, naming whoever they tell us to name. Send the requirements sheet over as soon as you have it, because the same exchange usually settles the lift and the dock window too.

Can we move the office over a weekend?

Weekend work is ordinary here, because our week has no closed days: 8AM to 6PM, Monday to Sunday, with Friday to Sunday priced at the weekend rate. Downtown the thing to plan for is parking, since county lots P3, P4 and P7 close on weekends and major holidays, which can move the loading point.

Can the move be split into stages?

It usually should be. We move departments in waves so part of the company keeps working while the rest is being carried out, and the inventory from the walkthrough decides how the waves are drawn. If a stage has to wait overnight, a loaded truck held until the next morning is $200.

Do you move server racks and IT equipment?

We move and place the hardware: screens, docks, printers and rack gear travel cable-tagged and padded, and every piece lands on the desk or rack position your IT contact has marked. Powering systems down, and bringing them back up, belongs to your own IT people, which keeps responsibility where the passwords are.

What if our new suite is far from the old one?

The rate card does not change with distance, but two rules do. Once the address is 30 miles or more from base the minimum becomes four hours rather than three, and beyond 10 miles the drive time counts double. Both are quoted before any deposit is taken.

Planning an Office Move Before Anyone Books a Truck

The truck is the last decision, not the first. What sets the cost of a business move is the inventory: how many desks, how many screens, how many filing cabinets, and how many of them are going to the new suite at all. A count taken two weeks out is worth more than any figure given over the phone.

Walk your own floor before you call anyone. Mark what is being kept, what the new furniture order replaces, and what is being sold or scrapped. Every item struck off that list before move day is time you do not pay for at an hourly rate, and space you do not rent twice.

Then fix the new floor plan on paper. Rooms, desk numbers and seat allocations let a crew put things where they belong instead of stacking them in a lift lobby for someone to sort out later. We ask for that plan at the walkthrough and the foreman carries a copy on the day.

Labels finish the job. Each desk gets a number, each carton carries that number plus a destination room, and a copy of the plan goes on the wall at the new address. It is a dull hour of work that turns an afternoon of questions into straight delivery.

Workstations, Records and the Equipment Nobody Wants to Carry Twice

Panel systems are the part most people underestimate. Cubicle walls come down in sequence, travel flat rather than upright, and go back together against the new plan; fixings are bagged and tagged by station so a rebuild is not a hunt for screws. Benching frames, sit-stand bases and monitor arms follow the same rule.

Technology travels with its cables tagged. Screens, docks, printers and rack-mounted gear go into the truck padded and upright, and come out at the desk or rack position your IT contact has marked, which is where our part of the job stops. Powering systems down, and bringing them back up, belongs to the people who hold the passwords.

Records need a decision before move day. Where a building will take the weight, lateral cabinets go loaded and locked; where it will not, drawers are boxed and numbered in order so nothing is refiled from scratch. Confidential files stay sealed and travel in one load rather than drifting across two.

Then there is the awkward item on every floor: a safe, a plotter, a reception counter built in place. Anything over 300 lbs is handled with a three-mover crew at $150 for the piece, and an office piano in a lobby or a staff lounge moves at $200 with a crew of four. Both are quoted before the day, once we have seen the item.

What the Building Will Ask For

Most delays we see are paperwork, not furniture. A management office wants a certificate of insurance naming the entities it lists, and it wants it before it will hand over a lift key. Ours costs $50 and goes out ahead of the crew, which is usually the same email that pins down the dock window.

Freight lift, dock and service corridor are booked together. Where there is no dock, the crew works from the nearest legal stopping point and carries, and that carry distance is part of what we time at the walkthrough. Padding goes on jambs, lift cabs and floors at both ends before anything rolls.

Downtown adds a layer worth knowing about. Downtown Santa Ana has been a National Register historic district since 1984, and in 1998 the city adopted Chapter 30 of its municipal code to establish the Santa Ana Register of Historical Properties. Improvements or alterations to a listed property, or to a contributing property in a historic district, must meet the Secretary of the Interior Standards for Rehabilitation and require a Certificate of Appropriateness.

For a mover that means one thing: nothing comes off the building. If a door leaf or a frame genuinely has to be removed to clear an oversized piece, that is a conversation for your property manager, not a decision for a crew on a Saturday. We plan the route around the opening we are given, and we say early when a piece will not fit through it.

Work in the street is separate again. The Public Works Agency Development Engineering office issues encroachment permits for work in the street or public right of way, and the permitted list includes lane or sidewalk closures supporting street, utility or maintenance work.

An applicant has to produce a City of Santa Ana business license, a certificate of insurance, a contractor’s license and approved plans. That is construction paperwork rather than anything a moving truck files for a morning at a kerb, and we will say so if the question comes up.

Keeping the Business Open While the Floor Moves

Nobody can afford a dark week, so we plan around the calendar you already have. Departments move in agreed waves: the group that can work from home or from a spare room goes first, the customer-facing desks go last, and the first wave is already logged in while the second is still labelling cartons.

Weekends carry most of that load. Our week has no closed days — 8AM to 6PM, Monday to Sunday — which makes a Saturday stage a normal booking at the weekend rate rather than a favour. What changes is the street: county lots P3, P4 and P7 shut on weekends and major holidays, so the loading point moves, not the plan.

Sometimes a stage cannot land the same day, because keys are late or a lift slot slips. A loaded truck held overnight is $200, and it is cheaper than unloading into a corridor and paying crew hours twice. Where the volume is larger than one load, a second truck at $200 keeps the whole floor moving in one pass.

Speed is possible when it has to be. Same-day work happens if a crew is free, and the number to ask on is 909-443-0004. A small suite with dock access is the best candidate for that; a full floor in a managed tower is not, because the lift and the insurance paperwork take their own time.

Furniture Apart, Across Town, and Back Together

Office furniture rarely survives a move in one piece unless it is taken apart properly. Conference tables lose their legs and bases, credenzas are emptied and wrapped, shelving is stripped and banded, and reception counters are unbolted where they were bolted. Everything travels blanket-wrapped, and the fixings travel labelled.

Reassembly is the half people forget to book time for. A crew that dismantled a workstation in the morning rebuilds it in the afternoon against your plan, levels it, and puts the chair and the screen back where the person using it expects to find them. That continuity is the reason we keep the same crew on both ends of a job.

Break rooms and kitchens deserve their own half hour of planning. Fridges need to be emptied and drained, coffee equipment disconnected by whoever services it, and dishwashers pulled by someone who is allowed to touch the plumbing. We carry the appliances; we do not pretend to be plumbers or electricians.
