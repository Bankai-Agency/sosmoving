What Happens From the First Call to the Last Strap

Most of a piano move happens before the instrument leaves the floor: measuring the route, clearing it and agreeing who carries which end.

When the crew arrives, the lead mover walks from the piano to the truck and notes every door, turn, threshold and step. You show where the piano should stand in the new home, and that spot is kept clear until the piano is brought in.

The lid and fallboard are closed, the case is wrapped in pads, and four movers lift it onto the board together. Uprights travel standing; grands lose their legs, pedal lyre and lid and go onto the board on their straight side. At delivery the order reverses, and the crew reassembles the piano in the room you picked.

Moving a Piano in Los Angeles: Curb, Building, Hill and Freeway

Parking & permits: holding the curb for a piano truck

A piano cannot be carried half a block, so the truck needs curb space at the door. Inside the City of Los Angeles, LADOT directs questions about requesting temporary no-parking signs to its Special Traffic Control Division, and we ask clients to make that call early. Timing matters as well: under Vehicle Code 22651(m) a car can be towed from reserved curb space only when the signs went up at least 24 hours before, so signs posted on the morning of the move clear nobody.

Yellow commercial zones allow just 30 minutes of loading, which a padded and strapped piano rarely fits into, so we never plan around them. In a preferential parking district, LADOT says a commercial vehicle parked for commercial activity needs no district permit, so the truck can load on a permit-only block; the crew still reads every sign on that block before stopping.

HOA, condo & building rules: elevators, gates and COI

Towers want paperwork before they want movers. Most Downtown LA high-rises ask for an approved Certificate of Insurance that lists both the building owner and its property manager as additional insured, and their service elevators are best booked two to three weeks ahead; we issue the COI for $50 and time the crew to the first minute of your booking.

The published move-out notice at Park La Brea has required reserved tower elevators, moves routed through the basement and a Monday-to-Saturday 8 AM to 5 PM window, and it warns that very large pieces may not fit the cars — so we measure the piano against the elevator in advance, and you confirm the current rules with management. Behind a staffed gate such as Mulholland Estates off Beverly Glen Boulevard, have the truck and crew cleared at the gatehouse the day before.

Area specifics: hillside streets, public stairs and narrow roads

The Eastside hills are where piano work gets physical. In Echo Park, many of the public staircases were built to help Angelenos get from Red Car stops up to their homes on the hill. When a house sits above or below the street, the crew carries the piano step by step on the board instead of rolling it.

LADOT notes that on hillside roads narrower than 24 feet two cars cannot pass once one side is occupied, so the truck stays at the curb only while it has to, with the piano already padded and boarded. Hillside segments in Glassell Park and Montecito Heights also carry Red Flag no-parking signs that are enforced on declared fire-weather days; we check the address and the LAFD alert before the crew rolls out.

Logistics & timing: routes, rush hours and the 8AM – 6PM day

Routing a loaded truck across LA is a job of its own. The Arroyo Seco Parkway, the old stretch of SR-110 toward Pasadena, is closed to trucks over 3 tons, so a piano bound for Highland Park or Mount Washington comes in by I-5 and surface streets, and we build that into the arrival time.

On boulevards posted as Anti-Gridlock or No Stopping zones, with Sunset Boulevard in Hollywood as a familiar example, the curb lane becomes a traffic lane at commute hours with no grace period, so we load outside the posted window or from a side street. The I-405 Sepulveda Pass between the Valley and the Westside ranks among the most congested corridors in the county, which is why we prefer a mid-day start for those moves. Same-day piano moves are possible inside the 8AM – 6PM working day.

Piano Moving Questions From LA Clients

Do I need a separate piano mover if my whole apartment is moving?

No. The piano rides with the rest of your household on one hourly move: you book a four-person crew, and the $200 piano add-on is added to that job. The 3-hour minimum applies to the whole move, not to the piano on its own.

Can you take a piano out of a Los Angeles high-rise or condo?

Yes, as long as the building releases a service or freight elevator for the move. Book the elevator window with management, send us the building’s insurance requirements, and we issue the COI for $50 ahead of time. We also check that the piano fits the elevator car before the crew arrives.

My house sits on a hillside with outdoor steps. Is that a problem?

It is common in this city and the reason we send four movers with a piano board. Tell us how many steps there are and send a photo of the path when you book. That helps us estimate the time and plan where the truck can stand on a narrow street.

Do you tune, store or dispose of pianos?

No. SOS moves pianos but does not tune, appraise, store or dispose of them. Book a tuner once the instrument has settled into its new room.

Can you move my piano today?

Often, yes: a same-day job is on the table if a crew of four has room in its schedule, so call 909-443-0004 as early as you can. We work Monday through Sunday, 8AM – 6PM, and the piano has to fit inside that window.

How is a piano move priced outside the city, for example in Orange County?

How a Los Angeles Piano Moving Company Plans the Job

A piano moving company in Los Angeles earns its fee on the phone, days before the truck is loaded. When you call, the office asks four things: what kind of piano it is, how many steps sit between it and the street, whether a building elevator or a guard gate is involved, and where a truck can legally stop. Those answers decide how long the crew will need and what has to be booked in advance.

We are Los Angeles piano movers in a particular sense: the instrument is handled by the same company, crew and truck that carry the rest of your home. When everything moves at once, you coordinate one arrival, one parking plan and one bill instead of two companies working around each other in a hallway.

The most useful thing you can send is a set of phone photos: the piano itself, the room it stands in, the door it will leave through, every flight of stairs and the street in front of the house. Five minutes of pictures often replaces a site visit, and the crew leader reads them before the truck leaves the yard.

If something on the day differs from the photos — a gate code that fails, a car left across the reserved curb — the crew leader calls you before improvising. Most surprises cost minutes when they are caught early and far more when the crew finds them with the piano already on the board.

Upright, Spinet, Baby Grand or Grand: What to Tell Us When You Book

You do not need the model number, but the office does need the category. The quickest test is shape. If the strings run vertically inside a cabinet standing against the wall, it is a vertical piano; if the body lies flat, has one curved side and a lid that props open, it is a grand of some size.

Vertical pianos come in several heights. A spinet or console is the short one, often no taller than a dresser, and people who look for spinet piano movers in Los Angeles are sometimes surprised that it still gets the full crew of four on stairs. Every piano, whatever its size, is booked with four movers.

If you need upright piano movers in Los Angeles for a studio or full-size cabinet, give us its approximate height. A tall upright is top-heavy, and its height tells the crew how far it can lean on a narrow stair before the carry becomes awkward.

Horizontal pianos are sorted by length. A baby grand is the compact version found in many living rooms, while parlor and concert grands are noticeably longer. Anyone booking baby grand piano movers in Los Angeles should mention the floor the piano stands on now and the room it is going to, because the crew reassembles it where it will stay.

When you compare grand piano movers in Los Angeles, ask each one how they will get the body through the tightest turn in your home and whether they want photos first. A long grand is judged by corners and landings more than by door width. For the handling itself — equipment, grip, ramps — our step-by-step guide to moving a piano goes further than this page.

Stairs, Narrow Doorways and Houses Above the Street

Ask any experienced piano mover in Los Angeles and you will hear the same thing: stairs do not rule out a piano move, they change its pace. Plenty of homes here put a flight of outdoor steps between the front door and the curb. On those flights the board is carried by four people who stop at each landing to reset their grip.

You can save the crew real time before they arrive. Clear shoes, plants and furniture off the stairs and the path, prop open gates, take down anything hanging low at a landing, and warn us about loose pavers or a step that wobbles. If an interior door is the narrowest point, measure its clear opening and add the number to your booking notes.

Floors get as much attention as the piano. Hardwood, tile and stone along the route are covered before the first lift, and the board carries the weight across them instead of the instrument’s small casters, which on older pianos were never meant to roll it across a room.

Condos swap outdoor steps for hallways and elevator cars. There the tight spot is usually the corner between the elevator lobby and the unit door, so we ask for its width along with the inside size of the elevator. The crew pads the elevator walls before the piano goes in.

What Sets the Time — and the Final Bill

Because the job is hourly, the bill follows the clock, and the clock follows access. These are the factors that move it the most:

  • Stairs and landings at the pickup and at the delivery address
  • Carrying distance from the truck to the door, including driveways and courtyards
  • Elevator bookings, gate check-in and loading dock rules
  • How much furniture and how many boxes travel with the piano
  • Drive time between the two addresses, counted double beyond 10 miles

People comparing cheap piano movers in Los Angeles sometimes see flat quotes well below the cost of an hourly crew of four. Ask what such a price includes: how many movers show up, whether they bring a board and straps, and what happens when the stairs turn out steeper than the quote assumed.

Affordable piano movers in Los Angeles are the ones whose price holds on move day, not the ones with the smallest number on paper. To keep your own hours down, have the path clear, the elevator booked, the gate cleared and the curb arranged before the crew arrives.

Long days have a rule of their own. A piano added to a large house move can push the job toward eight hours, and overtime applies after 8 hours, so for a big household we suggest an early start inside the 8AM – 6PM window.
