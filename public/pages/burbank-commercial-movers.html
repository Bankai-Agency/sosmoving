What Burbank Streets and Buildings Expect From an Office Move

Parking & permits: weekend curb space needs the city's approval

When a building has no dock, the truck works from the street, and in Burbank that curb space is covered by a Street Use Permit from Public Works. Weekends are where it gets strict. The permit rules do not allow weekend work without approval from the Public Works Director, and placement outside regular hours needs a city inspector paid at overtime rates, although the city can waive that inspection case by case.

Since most staged office moves fall on a Saturday or Sunday, we raise the curb question at the walkthrough. We hand your facilities contact the number of trucks, their size and the hours needed for the application, and you or the landlord file it early enough for the approval to arrive before the date.

Building rules: what a Media District tower expects

The Tower at 3900 W. Alameda Avenue shows what Burbank office landlords put in writing. Any company working in its buildings or using its loading dock must provide a certificate of insurance naming The Tower Burbank Owner, LLC and The Worthe Real Estate Group, Inc. as additional insured, and all work is scheduled through the management office. Tenant move-ins and move-outs must use the dock.

Crews are expected to bring Masonite for the lobby floors and to pad walls, corners and door frames along the route. A delivery that runs past 30 minutes or includes any piece over 5 feet has to be booked at least 48 hours ahead and handled outside business hours. For a tower like this we issue the $50 COI with those exact names, bring our own Masonite, and put long boardroom tables into a weekend slot within our 8AM – 6PM day.

Area specifics: the Media District, the Airport District and Avion

Three business areas produce three different kinds of move. The Media District is home to The Walt Disney Company's world headquarters, Warner Bros. Studios and ABC Television, and for offices in that district our plan starts with the building's dock, freight elevator and management-office bookings.

The Airport District around Hollywood Burbank Airport mixes high-tech companies, conference hotels and big-box retail, with Empire Center as its major power center; for a tech tenant we budget extra time for monitors, network gear and cable tagging. Avion Burbank at 3001 N. Hollywood Way is a 60-acre campus of 17 creative industrial and office buildings, where one move can mix desks with shelving and stock, and we quote both parts together.

Logistics & timing: the 4-to-6 p.m. peak and our working day

Burbank has tracked its commute peak for decades. The Transportation Demand Management ordinance, adopted for the Media District in 1991 and extended to Downtown in 1997, has employers with 25 or more staff track commute trips in the 4:00 to 6:00 p.m. peak period. Those are the last two hours of our crews' day, so on a single-day weekday move we aim to have the final load leave the old building before four, leaving the rest of the day for rebuilding desks and reconnecting screens at the new address.

Crews set out from our Jillson Street base in Los Angeles, and billing follows the usual hourly rules: a 3-hour minimum and drive time charged double beyond 10 miles. A small suite can often move the same day you call, if a crew is free.

Questions About Moving a Business in Burbank

Does our Burbank office need a city permit for the truck at the curb?

Only when the truck has to stand on the street; a building dock needs no city permit. Curb space is covered by a Public Works Street Use Permit, and a weekend move also needs approval from the Public Works Director. After the walkthrough we tell you whether your address needs one and give you the truck details for the application.

Our landlord wants a certificate of insurance before move day. Can you provide one?

Yes. We issue a COI for $50 with the additional-insured names your building specifies, as towers such as The Tower on W. Alameda Avenue require from anyone using their dock. Send us the landlord's requirements as soon as your date is set, and we deliver the certificate to the management office ahead of the move.

Can you move our office on a Saturday or Sunday?

Yes. Weekends are regular working days for our crews, always between 8AM and 6PM. Larger offices are usually split so one stage runs over a weekend while staff are away, and since Friday – Sunday rates are higher, we put less urgent loads on weekdays when your schedule allows.

How is the price of a Burbank office move worked out?

Hourly. Two movers with a truck start at $119 an hour cash, Monday to Thursday, and bigger crews cost more. Every job has a 3-hour minimum, plus $30 for gas and, if you need them, a $50 COI or a $200 second truck. You get a firm quote after we walk through the space.

Do you move warehouse and stockroom contents near the airport?

Yes: shelving units, stock cartons and the office furniture that goes with them. On campuses such as Avion Burbank, where office and industrial buildings share one site, we quote the stock area and the front office as a single job. Hazardous materials are the exception, and we send the list of items we cannot carry during planning.

Can a small Burbank office be moved on the day we call?

Often, yes, when a crew and truck are free that day. A small suite with a dock or an easy curb suits a same-day move best, because a weekend street permit or a management-office booking takes time to arrange. Phoning 909-443-0004 before noon gives the best odds.

Choosing Among Commercial Moving Companies in Burbank

Most commercial moving companies in Burbank can send three people and a truck to your door. The real differences show up earlier: in the questions asked before the quote, and in whether your landlord will let the crew through the lobby at all. A price given over the phone, without anyone seeing the space, says very little about the final bill.

Before signing with a commercial moving company in Burbank, check these points, and hold us to them as well:

  • Carrier registration you can look up yourself. Ours is USDOT 3398018, MC 1153871 and CAL-T0192140.
  • A certificate of insurance issued in your landlord's wording rather than a generic policy summary.
  • A walkthrough of both addresses before the price is fixed, followed by a firm quote in writing.
  • Working hours that fit your plan. Our crews work Monday – Sunday, 8AM – 6PM, and we do not schedule moves outside that window.
  • Rates listed per crew size, for weekdays and weekends separately, so two differently timed stages can be compared side by side.

Timing belongs in the comparison too. A small suite can often be moved on the day you call, but a staged move for a larger office needs lead time for the landlord's bookings, the certificate of insurance and any weekend approval from Public Works, so start the conversation as soon as a move date is on the table.

How the service is organized across the county is explained on our commercial and office moving page. The rest of this guide covers what changes once the job is in Burbank.

Start With an Inventory, Not a Truck Booking

An office move is sized by what is in the rooms, and the people who work there know that better than any estimator. Before the walkthrough, walk the floor with a notepad: count workstations, filing cabinets, conference tables, printers and anything plugged into the network. Mark what is staying behind, because furniture you do not plan to take should be decided before the crew arrives, not on the day.

Next, build a seat map for the new space. Give each desk a code, for example a floor letter plus a number, and assign every employee to one. Our crew labels cartons, chairs and monitors with the same codes, so unloading becomes a matter of matching labels to the plan taped to each door.

Measure the destination as well. A boardroom table or reception counter that fit the old suite may not clear a tighter corridor or the freight elevator at the new building, so ask building management for the elevator cab dimensions and send them to us; we check the largest pieces against them before the date.

Technology needs a list of its own:

  • Workstations and monitors, noting who uses a docking station.
  • Shared printers, copiers and conference-room screens.
  • Network cabinets and server equipment, and who at your company or your IT vendor is responsible for them.
  • Leased equipment, since the leasing company may want to move it itself.

We disconnect and reconnect computers as part of the moving work and tag each cable to its machine. Network settings, phone systems and software stay with your IT people; our job is to get the hardware to the right desk undamaged and plugged back in. A fuller planning list is in our office moving checklist.

What Your Lease and Building Handbook Will Ask For

Ask the management office at both addresses for its move or contractor procedures as soon as the lease is signed, and forward them to us. Burbank landlords differ in the details, but four subjects come up again and again.

  • Insurance. The landlord names the entities that must appear as additional insured. At The Tower on W. Alameda Avenue, for example, that means the owner entity and The Worthe Real Estate Group. Our $50 certificate goes straight to the management office.
  • The loading dock. Some buildings require every tenant move to go through the dock. That settles where the truck parks, but the slot has to be booked.
  • Oversized pieces. Long boardroom tables and reception counters may fall under advance-booking rules; The Tower asks for 48 hours' notice when any single piece is over 5 feet.
  • Protection. Expect a requirement for floor protection in the lobby and padding on walls, corners and door frames. Our crews carry Masonite sheets and pads as standard equipment.

A building with a dock usually keeps the city out of the picture, because the truck never stands in the street. Without one, two sets of rules apply at once, the landlord's and the city's, and we line up both approvals side by side so that neither one holds up a weekend date.

Your old space matters as well. Leases often expect the suite handed back empty, so decide early what happens to furniture you are not taking, and tell us, so it stays off the inventory and out of the quote.

Keeping the Business Open While You Move

Very few companies can close for the days a large move takes. The alternative is a staged relocation, in which the office moves in parts and each part is short enough to finish inside one working day. Since our working week runs Monday – Sunday, 8AM – 6PM, stages can fall on weekdays, weekends or both.

A typical sequence for an office relocation in Burbank looks like this:

  1. Archives, storage rooms and spare furniture go first, on a weekday, when the lower Monday – Thursday rates apply.
  2. Working departments follow on a Saturday or Sunday, one or two teams per stage, so staff close their desks on Friday and open them at the new address on Monday.
  3. Shared rooms such as the conference room, break room and reception move together with the last department.
  4. The server room and network cabinet go last, timed with your IT team so systems are offline for as short a period as possible.

Each stage gets its own room list, crew size and truck count. The building managers receive the same list, which makes it simple to book the dock and the freight elevator for every date. If a stage finishes early, the crew spends the remaining hours rebuilding desks and reconnecting equipment at the new address.

Think about the first working day at the new address, too. It can pay to book a small crew for a few hours on the Monday after the final stage, to shift desks that landed in the wrong spot and collect anything left at the old suite. That visit is billed like any other job, so the 3-hour minimum applies to it.

Furniture, Technology and Records on Moving Day

Each stage runs in a fixed order. Items are wrapped and labeled in their rooms, furniture is taken apart where needed, and the truck is loaded so that what the new floor needs first comes off first. Desks and cubicle panels travel flat, covered with blankets and stretch wrap, with their hardware bagged and taped to the matching piece.

A few parts of an office get particular handling:

  • Screens and computers go into monitor cartons or anti-static wrap, and towers ride upright.
  • Filing cabinets sometimes move with the files inside, depending on the cabinet and the elevator; otherwise the contents go into archive boxes in drawer order.
  • Break-room appliances such as refrigerators and coffee stations should be emptied and cleaned by your staff the day before.
  • Printers and copiers travel padded with their paper trays emptied; a leased machine waits for the leasing company if its contract says so.
  • Conference-room tables come apart into top and base, and the video-conference hardware is packed separately from the furniture.

Personnel files, client records and anything confidential are best packed by your own staff in sealed, numbered cartons, with the list kept by the office manager. The crew keeps those cartons together on the truck and carries them straight to the room you name, without opening anything.

At the new address the crew rebuilds workstations according to the seat map, reconnects the computers and sets each carton at the desk it belongs to. We also take away the packing materials we brought, so your team does not begin the week behind a wall of empty boxes.

How Burbank Commercial Movers Put a Price on the Job

We price office work in Burbank the same way we price every local job: by the hour, for a crew with a truck. The rate is set by crew size and by the day of the week, with Monday to Thursday cheaper than Friday to Sunday and cash cheaper than card. The full rate card is higher up on this page.

The number of hours comes from the job itself:

  • How much there is to move, and how much of it has to be taken apart.
  • Access at both ends: a dock with a freight elevator is faster than a street curb with a passenger lift.
  • Distance: once a job sits 30 or more miles out, the minimum is 4 hours, and beyond 10 miles each hour of driving counts double.
  • The number of stages: each one is booked as its own job with its own minimum, so fewer, fuller stages usually cost less.
  • Extras: $30 gas, $50 for a certificate of insurance, $200 for a second truck, with overtime counted after the eighth hour.

A lower bill does not always mean a smaller crew. Four movers cost more per hour than two but can empty a suite in far fewer hours, and at the walkthrough we tell you which option comes out cheaper for your space.

When you compare quotes, put them on the same footing: the same number of movers, the same days and the same access at both ends. A price that assumes a dock at a building that only offers a curb, or a weekday slot at a tower that wants long pieces moved outside business hours, will not survive the actual move.

Our Crew's Checklist for Your Team

Office managers often ask what they should handle themselves. The short answer is decisions and access, while the lifting stays with us. Before each stage, please make sure that:

  • one person on your side can answer questions and approve changes during the day;
  • keys, access cards or building passes for the crew are arranged with the management office;
  • employees have packed personal items and cleared their desk drawers;
  • the seat map is final and a copy is taped at the entrance of the new floor;
  • anything that must not be moved is clearly marked.

Everything else, from wrapping the first chair to rebuilding the last desk, is work for our crew. That division of labor is the point of hiring office movers in Burbank rather than asking staff to carry boxes on a Saturday.
