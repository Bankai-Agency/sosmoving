When a Last Minute Move Can’t Wait

Almost nobody who calls a last minute moving company planned it that way. A date moved, a promise fell through, a job came up — and the move had to follow.

These are the situations we hear most often from people looking for last minute movers in Los Angeles:

  • The lease ends first. The old place has to be handed back on a fixed day, and the new one is ready on the same day or not at all.
  • A closing shifted. Escrow moved the date, and the keys change hands earlier than anyone planned.
  • Another company dropped out. The movers you booked cancelled or never showed up, and the truck you counted on is gone.
  • A new job starts Monday. The commute from the old address does not work, and waiting another month is not an option.
  • Family comes first. Someone needs help nearby, or someone has to move in with you this week.
  • An eviction date. We handle the move itself; the legal side is a question for an attorney, not for movers.

Some people search for emergency movers in these moments. None of it needs a long story on the phone: what helps is the date that cannot move, both addresses and a rough idea of how much is going.

What to Get Ready in the Next Few Hours

On an hourly job, every minute you save before the crew arrives comes straight off the bill. These steps save the most.
  • Small things into boxes or bags. Loose items on shelves cost a crew more minutes than any sofa. Stack whatever is packed near the door.
  • Beds apart, or say so. Take them apart yourself, or tell us on the call that the crew should do it, so they come ready for it.
  • Floors and elevators. Tell us the floor at both addresses, whether there is an elevator and whether it has to be reserved.
  • A place for the truck. A driveway, an open stretch of curb, a loading dock. The closer the truck stands, the shorter every trip.
  • Name the heavy pieces. Mention anything over 300 lbs before we dispatch: it costs $150 extra and needs a crew of three. A piano is $200 with a crew of four; see how we move pianos.

If only half of this is done when the crew rings the bell, that is normal on a move like this. Tell the crew lead what is left, and they start with whatever is ready.

Same Day Moving in Los Angeles: Curb, Building, Hill

Three things in this city decide whether a move can start the minute the crew arrives: where the truck stops, what the building allows, and how steep and narrow the street is.

Parking the truck without a permit

California Vehicle Code 22651(m) allows towing from a posted temporary no-parking zone only once the signs have been up for 24 hours. For a move today, the curb in front of your door is whatever it is right now. LADOT’s yellow loading zones help with a short stop — a commercial vehicle may stay up to 30 minutes, Monday to Saturday from 7AM to 6PM unless the signs say otherwise — but a whole apartment rarely fits into that window.

In a preferential parking district, the truck does not need a district permit while it is working. On boulevards with anti-gridlock tow-away signs the parking lane becomes a traffic lane during the posted peak hours, with no grace period at either end, so we load before or after that window or from a side street. The crew asks where the nearest legal spot is and, if it is far, plans the carry before the first box goes out.

Buildings: elevator windows, insurance, house rules

Managed towers and condo buildings across Los Angeles commonly want a certificate of insurance on file, naming the building, before they release a service elevator or a loading dock. Some complexes also limit moves to set hours and do not allow them on Sundays or holidays at all.

On a same-day move this is the part nobody can rush: the building decides. Call management first and ask three questions — is the elevator free today, what insurance wording do you need, which hours are allowed. Send us the answers, we prepare the COI for $50, and the crew is timed to the start of your elevator window.

Hills, narrow streets and Red Flag days

LADOT notes that on hillside streets narrower than 24 feet two vehicles cannot pass once something is parked on one side, and a moving truck is exactly that something. On declared Red Flag days, posted no-parking segments in the hillside fire zones are enforced by towing; whether an address falls in one can be checked at neighborhoodinfo.lacity.org under “Red Flag Parking Zone.”

For a move up in the hills we check the address first, keep the truck’s time at the curb short and bring things out to the loading point in batches. Trucks over 3 tons are banned from the Arroyo Seco Parkway, so for Highland Park or Mount Washington we route around it and count that in when we name the window.

Same Day Moving Questions

Can you move me today?

Often, yes — if a crew is free when you call. Call 909-443-0004 with both addresses and a rough idea of the volume, and you will know on the same call whether today works or when the next opening is.

Until what time can I call for a move today?

Any time during our working hours, Monday – Sunday, 8AM – 6PM, as long as a crew is still free; there is no separate cutoff. The job itself also has to fit into the working day, so a call late in the afternoon may turn into a booking for the next morning.

Do you move at night or on weekends?

Not at night: our crews work from 8AM to 6PM. Weekends are regular working days for us, billed at the Friday – Sunday rates from the table above.

Is a same-day move more expensive?

No. A move booked for today is billed at the same hourly rates, minimums and fees as any other local move — there is no surcharge for short notice.

Can you do a long-distance move on short notice?

Yes, when a crew and a truck are free. The price is a flat rate, worked out the same way as for any long-distance move, and the transit time depends on the route.

My new place is not ready yet. What happens to my things?

An overnight hold costs $200 and lets us deliver the next day. For a longer gap between two addresses, your things can go into storage until you have the keys. Tell us on the first call, because it changes the plan for the day.

Does my building need a certificate of insurance?

Many managed buildings ask for one. Get their exact requirements from management — the name to list, the coverage and the allowed hours — and send them to us; we prepare the COI for $50. On a move today, the building’s answer often sets the start time.

How many movers do I need?

It depends on the volume, the stairs and the heaviest pieces. Anything over 300 lbs needs a crew of at least three, and a piano needs four; for everything else the office suggests a crew size on the call, and you confirm it before we dispatch.

A 72-Hour Plan for a Last-Minute Move in Los Angeles

Three days sounds like nothing when a whole apartment has to go. It is enough, though, if the steps come in the right order. Most trouble in last minute moving does not come from packing too slowly. It comes from doing things in the wrong sequence: boxing up the kitchen before anyone has asked the building about the elevator, or booking a truck before knowing whether the new place will be ready.

The plan below counts from the moment you learn you have to move and assumes the crew comes on day three. It works whether you hire last minute moving services for everything or carry part of the load yourself. Is the move today? Then jump to the section about moving day. Tomorrow? Start with the calls.

Hours 0–3: Settle the Facts Before the First Box

Before you touch a single box, write down four things: the date and hour you must be out, the date and hour you can get in, both addresses with floor numbers, and who holds the keys at each end. If the two dates do not meet, you already know you need a place for your things in between. Better to learn that now than at five in the afternoon on moving day.

Then decide what is not coming. On an hourly move every item is carried twice, out of the old place and into the new one. The sofa you meant to replace anyway, the bookcase that never fit, the boxes from the last move that were never opened: sell them, give them away or leave them for a donation pickup. Our tips for packing on short notice go through that sorting in more detail.

One more decision belongs to the first hours: who does what. If two adults are moving, split the list now — one takes the calls, the other takes the rooms. Doing both at once is how whole afternoons disappear.

Hours 3–12: The Calls That Cannot Wait Until Tomorrow

Some calls only work during business hours, so make them on the first day. Start with the building you are leaving: tell management the date and ask what the move-out inspection involves. Then the building you are moving into. Then the employer or the school, if the new address changes anything there.

Next come the services that follow you: internet, power, gas and the mail. Transfers take time on the provider’s side, and a request filed on day one has a far better chance of being done by day three than one filed the night before. The same goes for the change of address with the post office.

Keep a single note, on paper or in your phone, with every confirmation, name and reference number. On moving day someone will ask for at least one of them, usually at the worst possible moment.

If the movers are not booked yet, this is the time. Next day movers and crews for later in the week are much easier to find than a crew for this afternoon, and every hour of waiting narrows the choice.

Day Two: Pack for the Truck, Not for Show

The second day belongs to packing. Start with the rooms you use least — the storage closet, the guest room, the bookshelves — and finish with the kitchen and the bathroom, which you need until the last evening. Label each box on the side, not the top, with the room it is going to. Stacked boxes hide their lids.

Heavy things go into small boxes and light things into large ones. A large box full of books is a box nobody can lift safely, and it slows the crew more than two small ones would. Fill empty corners with towels and linens instead of buying more paper.

Photograph the back of the TV and the computer before you unplug anything; in the new place those photos save an hour of guessing. Put the screws and bolts from anything you take apart into a zip bag and tape it to the piece it came from.

Set aside one box per person marked “open first”: a change of clothes, chargers, medication, basic toiletries, a towel. Documents, jewelry and anything you cannot replace travel with you, not in the truck. If packing the whole home yourself is simply not realistic, ask about professional packing when you book the move.

The Night Before: Keys, Payment and a Plan B

The evening before the move is for loose ends, not for a packing marathon. Confirm who meets the crew at each address, and give that person the gate code, the parking notes and your phone number. If you will not be at the new place yourself, say so when you book rather than when the truck is already on its way.

Decide how you are going to pay. Our rates differ between cash and card, and that is easier to settle the night before than at the end of a long day.

Then think through a plan B. What happens if the new apartment is not cleaned in time, or the keys come at 4PM instead of 10AM? The answer might be a friend’s garage, a later delivery or storage for the days between two leases. Having that answer ready turns a crisis on moving day into one more phone call.

And sleep. It sounds trivial, yet a tired person makes slow decisions, and on an hourly job slow decisions are the ones you pay for.

Moving Day: Where Your Attention Pays Off

When the crew arrives, walk through the home once with the crew lead. Point out what is fragile, what is heavy, what stays behind and what goes to storage instead of the new address. Five minutes here prevents most of the questions that would otherwise interrupt the work later.

After that, the most useful thing you can do is stay reachable and out of the carrying path. Use the time to empty the fridge, strip the bedding and check closets, cabinets and the space behind doors, the places where things are most often left behind.

Before the last load leaves, take photos of the empty rooms. If a deposit or a move-out inspection is involved, those pictures are your record of the condition you left the place in.

At the new address, stand where the furniture comes in and name the room for each piece as it passes. Furniture placed right the first time does not need a second trip across the room at an hourly rate.

The First Day After: Unpack in the Right Order

Unpack for the first night, not for the first dinner party. Beds and bedding come first, then the bathroom, then just enough of the kitchen to make coffee. Everything else can wait a day or two without anyone noticing.

Go through the furniture and the boxes while the move is still fresh in your memory. If something is damaged or missing, write it down with photos and tell the mover promptly; a problem reported weeks later is harder to reconstruct for everyone. Keep the booking confirmation and the final bill in the same folder as the new lease.

When Three Days Are Not Enough

Sometimes the honest answer is that three days will not cover everything. Then split the job. Move the essentials on the fixed date — beds, clothes, the kitchen basics — and bring the rest over on a second day, or send it to storage and sort it out later.

A second day is a second booking with its own minimum, so splitting is not free. It is usually cheaper, though, than paying a crew to wait while half a home is still being packed. Not sure which way your move leans? Describe the situation on the phone, and the office will tell you how moves like yours usually play out.

Urgent movers are easier to find than most people think. The harder part is arriving at moving day with a plan, and the order above holds whether the truck comes this afternoon or in three days: facts first, calls second, boxes third.
